MailGrail turns React components into email templates your backend renders on
its own. You write MJML with full TypeScript types, preview it live in a
browser, and compile it to a portable template, a render function, and its type
declarations.

The compiled output depends on your templating engine and nothing else. No
React, no MJML, no bundler, and no MailGrail in production — all of that work
happens at build time.

## One schema, three jobs

A template declares its parameters once, with a compact DSL:

import Snippet from "../../../../components/docs/Snippet.astro";

<Snippet id="welcome-schema" title="emails/WelcomeEmail.tsx" />

That single declaration does three separate jobs:

- it **types the template** you write, so `mg.render("usrname")` is a compile
  error rather than a blank space in a sent email;
- it **generates placeholder data** for the preview server, so you see a
  realistic email without wiring up a database;
- it **emits the `.d.ts`** your backend imports, so the call site knows exactly
  which parameters are required and which may be left out.

Rename a parameter and all three move with it. That is the whole idea.

## What you get out

`mailgrail build` writes three files per template:

```
emails-dist/
  confirm-email.ejs     full HTML with placeholders, for reading and debugging
  confirm-email.js      the render function — inlines the template, imports only your engine
  confirm-email.d.ts    the parameter types
```

Your backend imports the `.js`, calls it with typed parameters, and hands the
result to whatever sends your mail.

## When this is a good fit

MailGrail suits transactional email that ships from a backend — confirmations,
receipts, invitations, password resets — where you want the authoring
experience of components and the deployment profile of a plain string template.

It is not a campaign tool and has no sending infrastructure. It produces the
`subject`, `text` and `html`; delivery is yours, through nodemailer, Resend,
SES or anything else.
